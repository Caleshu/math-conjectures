## Original conjecture

For every uncompressible type-A interval $[x,w]$ with connected transposition graph and odd length $d$, if $G(x,w)=0$, then
\[
 [q^{(d-1)/2}]P_{x,w}(q)\leq1.
\]

**Normalized statement.** For every integer $m\geq1$ and every $x\leq w$ in the Bruhat order on $S_m$, let $d=\ell(w)-\ell(x)$ and let $c$ be the number of cycles of $x^{-1}w$, including fixed points. If $d$ is odd, $(x,w)$ is uncompressible, its transposition graph is connected, and $d-m-c+2=0$, then $[q^{(d-1)/2}]P_{x,w}(q)\leq1$.

## Definitions

- **$G(x,w)$**: For a connected transposition graph on $m$ positions, $G(x,w)=(d-m-c+2)/2$, where $d$ is the difference of the endpoint inversion numbers and $c$ counts all cycles of the relative permutation.
- **Uncompressible type-A interval**: The endpoints are permutations in $S_m$. No position $i$ has a common value $j=x(i)=w(i)$ with equal counts of entries at least $j$ before $i$ in the two permutations.
- **Connected transposition graph**: The position transpositions along a saturated Bruhat chain from $x$ to $w$ form the edges of a connected graph on $\{1,\ldots,m\}$. Connectedness is independent of the chain.
- **Top permitted coefficient**: For odd $d>0$, the degree bound for the Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial permits degree at most $(d-1)/2$. The specified coefficient is zero if the actual degree is smaller.

### [P-eda322] Factor the interval along the position components of a saturated chain.

By agent:construction. Status **review_passed**, level independently reviewed proof (AI reviewer).

**Target:** The component factorization and uncompressibility used in C2.

Fix a saturated chain and let $B_1,\ldots,B_k$ be its position components. These blocks are the same for every saturated chain. Indeed, maximal chains of a Bruhat interval are connected by diamond moves. In a rank-two diamond the two transpositions on either path factor the same permutation. For disjoint transpositions the two supports are determined by the product. For overlapping transpositions either path connects the same three positions. A diamond move therefore preserves the component partition. Every interval element lies on a saturated chain, so its values in $B_i$ form the fixed set $x(B_i)$. Restricting a saturated chain to $B_i$ and flattening gives a chain of covers there, since deleting other positions cannot introduce an entry that blocks a cover. Conversely, assemble any tuple of permutations lying between the flattened endpoints on the respective blocks. Every northwest rank of the assembled permutation is the sum of the corresponding block ranks. The Bruhat rank inequalities put it between $x$ and $w$. These arguments also apply to comparisons within the interval, so it is the product of the flattened intervals. To obtain polynomial factorization, use Dyer's increasing-path formula for the normalized $R$-polynomials with lexicographic reflection order on position transpositions. Every directed transposition edge changes just one block, and its orientation agrees with the orientation after flattening. Given increasing paths in the blocks, sorting their transposition labels gives the unique increasing interleaving. Their path lengths add, so the normalized $R$-polynomials multiply on every subinterval. The ordinary $R$-polynomials then multiply because the rank differences add. The defining Kazhdan–Lusztig inversion relation factors over the product, and the product of the component Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials satisfies the degree bound. Uniqueness gives the claimed factorization. Finally, if a position $i$ with common value $j$ were compressible, the endpoint prefix counts at thresholds $j$ and $j+1$ would agree at both prefix lengths $i-1$ and $i$. The rank inequalities force the same four counts for every intermediate permutation, so each has value $j$ at $i$. This isolates $i$ on every saturated chain. A connected interval of positive length is consequently uncompressible.
