## Original conjecture

We also verified up to $n=10$ that the lollipop graph maximizes the HT index over the class of all non-isomorphic connected simple graphs by generating every such graph with the \texttt{geng} program from the Nauty and Traces package \cite{geng}. For $n>10$ we found the maximum among lollipop graphs only; whether these values are the global maxima remains an open question.

**Normalized statement.** For every $n\ge 6$ (or all sufficiently large $n$), is $\max\{HT(G): G \text{ a simple connected graph on } n \text{ vertices}\}$ attained by a lollipop graph $L(n,k)$ for some $k$? The paper further suggests that asymptotically the maximizer is $L(n,k)$ with $k\approx 0.558n$, i.e., the old conjecture (maximizer $=P_n$) should be replaced by the lollipop $L(n,.558n)$.

## Definitions

- **$E_iT_j$**: Expected hitting time of vertex $j$ by simple random walk on $G$ started at $i$, where $T_j=\inf\{n\ge0: X_n=j\}$ and the walk moves to a uniformly random neighbor.
- **Hitting time index**: $HT(G)=\sum_{i<j}\max\{E_iT_j,E_jT_i\}$ for a simple connected undirected graph $G$.
- **Lollipop graph $L(n,k)$**: A $k$-clique with an $(n-k)$-path attached through an extra edge to a vertex of the clique; it has $n$ vertices.

Brightwell–Winkler: the n-vertex graph with the largest single hitting time is a lollipop with clique size about 2n/3 and value about 4n^3/27. - *Further results on the expected hitting time, the cover cost and the related invariants of graphs* (2017, arXiv:1707.02002): technique. ## Failed approaches

- **Use the unweighted path bound $W(G)\le W(P_n)$ independently of the edge count.** (extremal): Together with $2m\le n(n-1)$ this gives leading coefficient $1/6$ for $HT(G)/n^5$. The sharp coefficient is $f(\alpha)$, leaving a constant gap of $1/6-f(\alpha)$. The discarded information is the loss of path cuts forced by a positive edge density.
- **Lemma: given a core $H$ on $k$ vertices with a pendant path of length $L$ at $v$, $H=K_k$ maximises HT** (strategist): False: if $H$ is itself a lollipop attached at its path end, the result is a longer lollipop, which beats $K_k$ when $k$ is far from optimal. The term $L\sum_u E_uT_v^H$ can be of order $n^5$. Requiring 2-connectivity does not save it (a clique with a long ear has hitting times of order $k^3$).
- **Bound $W$ via the maximum transmission, $W\le\frac n2\max_x\sigma(x)$, or via breadth-first layers from one vertex** (strategist): Loses a constant factor (the lollipop's path end has $\sigma\approx L^2/2+kL$, much more than the average), and breadth-first layers do not control same-layer distances (cycles).
- **Bound $W$ only in terms of the counts of high- and low-degree vertices $(h,\ell)$** (strategist): Barbells have larger $W$ for the same $(h,\ell)$; the trade-off only appears once edges $m\approx\sum h_i^2/2$ are taken into account.
- **The paper's bound $HT\le 2m\,Kf(P_n)$** (strategist): Gives about $n^5/6$, roughly 8 times the true constant $c^*\approx0.02145$.
- **Attach the tail to the clique through several edges (path start adjacent to r>1 clique vertices).** (construction): With r exits, the clique→tail crossing time drops from about $k^2$ to about $k^2/r$ for every clique–tail pair (a $\Theta(n^4)$ loss), while the extra edges add only $O(n^3)$.
- **Interpolate between L(n,k) and L(n,k+1) with a partially attached vertex (path-complete-type graph), hoping to beat both when the real optimum $\alpha^* n$ falls between integers.** (construction): The leading-order HT is $A+B/r+Cr$, which is convex in r, so the maximum is at an endpoint (a lollipop).
- **Barbell or asymmetric barbell (second clique at the far end of the path).** (construction): HT is governed by the larger clique's edge count times distance. Vertices in the second clique contribute less than path vertices would. The symmetric barbell's leading constant is ≤ about 0.0089 < 0.02145.
- **Tree tails (brooms, spiders, two tails) or pendants on the clique instead of a single path.** (construction): Each pair's max hitting time is about $k^2$ times a quantity ≤ the tail distance, and the path maximizes the Wiener index among trees. Pendants on the clique act like clique vertices but add far fewer edges.
- **Add chords on the path or a small cycle at the path's far end to increase |E|.** (construction): Local resistance drops, cutting crossing times worth $\Theta(|E|)=\Theta(n^2)$ for $\Theta(n^2)$ pairs, while one extra edge adds only $O(n^3)$ in total.
- **Bound the dense-side hitting times in the clique-completion comparison by commute times, $E^A\le2m_1R^A\le2k^2/t$** (extremal): This overestimates by a factor of about 2, since $E\approx k$ while the bound is $\approx2k$. The per-pair loss becomes $\Theta(k)$, comparable to the gain, so it only shows $|F|=O(1/(\gamma\beta))$, roughly $\le30$ missing edges, not $|F|=0$. Two-sided resistance estimates fix this.
- **Rule out low-degree vertices on the dense side by turning such a vertex into a clique vertex, bounding its pairs by $\max\le$ commute time** (extremal): Every term involved is of order $n^3$: the gain $\approx kb^2$, the loss $2m_2\sum R_{cw}\approx b^3$, and the loss $2mbR_{vc}$. Replacing max by the sum of both directions throws away a quantity of the same order. So gap (R1) needs the exact $\rho$-ordering or finer hitting-time estimates.
- **Prove the tail is a tree by bounding the internal $G_2$ terms by $2m_2Kf(G_2)$** (extremal): $2m_2Kf(G_2)\sim b^4/3$ swamps the gain $2m_1(W(P)-Kf(G_2))\sim k^2b$ for tails close to a path. The internal comparison is again an HT-type problem for $G_2$, which fails since lollipops beat paths. The large weight at $c$ has to be used, not discarded.
