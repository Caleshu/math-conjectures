## Failed approaches

- **Construct a pair by specializing consecutive nonconstant polynomials whose irreducible factors all occur at least three times.** (strategist): Over $\mathbb Q$, let $F$ have degree $d>0$. If every root of $F$ and $F+1$ had multiplicity at least three, the two disjoint root sets together would have at most $2d/3$ distinct elements. At a root of multiplicity $m$, their common derivative $F'$ vanishes to order $m-1$. Its degree would therefore be at least $2d-2d/3=4d/3$, contradicting $\deg F'=d-1$. Individual integer specializations can still acquire repeated prime factors.
- **Use $abc$ alone to decide existence.** (strategist): C6 gives a conditional bound depending on $K_{1/4}$. Finiteness by itself permits a nonempty exceptional set, and the argument supplies neither a witness nor a numerical search limit.
- **Use a prime appearing for the first time in the Pell sequence to force a new divisor of the index.** (construction): First appearance does not determine multiplicity.
