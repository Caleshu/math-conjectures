## Original conjecture

{\em Characterize the chromatic vectors of Hamiltonian cycles of the hypercube.} Or, in other words, {\em characterize the image of the map $c:\{\mbox{Hamiltonian cycles}\}\to\N^n$.}

**Normalized statement.** For each $n\ge2$, determine exactly which vectors $(c_0,\dots,c_{n-1})\in\mathbb{N}^n$ arise as $c(h)$ for some Hamiltonian cycle $h$ of $Q_n$, where $c_i$ is the number of edges of $h$ in dimension $i$.

## Source

Feliú Sagols, Guillermo Morales-Luna, *Parity balance of the $i$-th dimension edges in Hamiltonian cycles of the hypercube* (2010), arXiv:1009.3304, doi:10.48550/arxiv.1009.3304

## Definitions

- **chromatic vector $c(h)$**: $c(h)=(c_i)_{i=0}^{n-1}$, where $c_i$ counts the vertices $h_\iota$ whose outgoing cycle edge $h_\iota h_{\iota+1}$ lies in dimension $i$, i.e. the number of $i$-th dimension edges of $h$.

- **computational** (iteration 1, plan): Plan. I test the listed necessary conditions (entries even, each at least 2, sum $2^n$, largest at most $2^{n-1}$) against a stronger condition. That condition is proved below.

**Projection lemma (proved_here).** Let $h$ be a Hamiltonian cycle of $Q_n$ and $S\subseteq\{0,\dots,n-1\}$ with $|S|=k\ge1$. Deleting the coordinates outside $S$ turns $h$ into a closed walk in $Q_k$:
- each dimension-$i$ edge with $i\in S$ becomes one step in dimension $i$;
- every other edge becomes a pause.

So the walk has length $\sum_{i\in S}c_i$. It visits every vertex of $Q_k$, because every fiber contains vertices of $h$. A closed walk that visits all $2^k$ vertices has length at least $2^k$. Hence $\sum_{i\in S}c_i\ge 2^k$.

**Tight case.** If $\sum_{i\in S}c_i=2^k$ and $k\ge2$, the walk visits each vertex exactly once, so it is a Hamiltonian cycle of $Q_k$. Then $(c_i)_{i\in S}$ must itself be a chromatic vector of $Q_k$.

**Condition (P).** Sort the entries in increasing order: $s_1\le\dots\le s_n$. (P) says $s_1+\dots+s_k\ge 2^k$ for every $k$, with equality at $k=n$.
- (P) is strictly stronger than the listed conditions. For $n=5$, the vector $(2,2,2,10,16)$ meets all the listed conditions but fails (P), since $2+2+2<8$. So the listed conditions are **not** sufficient for $n\ge5$.
- Taking $k=n-1$ in (P) gives the bound largest $\le 2^{n-1}$ again.
- Refined condition (P+): (P) plus the tight-case requirement, applied recursively.

- **extremal** (iteration 2, main): I proved Lemma B rigorously and found an exact test for its orientation hypothesis. The rule. Process the classes in order and let $\sigma_L$ be the sum of $\sigma$ over the classes already processed. Inside an odd-valued class, $\sigma$ alternates and starts with $+1$ if $\sigma_L=0$ or with $-1$ if $\sigma_L=1$. A class of size $T$ changes $\sigma_L$ by $\pm[T\text{ odd}]$, the sign pointing back toward the range, so $\sigma_L$ stays in $\{0,1\}$.

Final total. $\sigma([m])\equiv\#\{i:D_i\text{ odd}\}\equiv D([m])=2^m\equiv0\pmod 2$, so $\sigma([m])=0$ and $X([m])=Y([m])=2^{m-1}$.

Order lemma. If $D_a<D_b$ then $X_a\le\lceil D_a/2\rceil\le\lfloor D_b/2\rfloor\le X_b$, and likewise for $Y$. Sort the indices by $(X_i,D_i)$; the result is still non-decreasing in $D$. The same holds for $Y$.

Parity reformulation. Put $\varphi(t)=P_k-2^k=D(L)+tw-2^{l+t}$. Then $X(Q)\ge2^{k-1}$ iff $\varphi(t)\ge-\sigma(Q)$, and $Y(Q')\ge2^{k-1}$ iff $\varphi(t)\ge\sigma(Q')$. Case 1: $t=T$, or $w$ even. Then $|\sigma(Q)|\le1$ and $\varphi\ge0$, so (*) applies.

The $t$ smallest $X$-entries take the $-1$ signs first, and the $t$ smallest $Y$-entries take the $+1$ signs first. The requirements are therefore at most $\min(t,f)-\sigma_L$ for $X$ and $\min(t,c)+\sigma_L$ for $Y$. If $\sigma_L=0$ then $c=\lceil T/2\rceil$ and $f=\lfloor T/2\rfloor$. ## Failed approaches

- **Gluing two copies of the same cycle (or translates) in the two layers, deleting several common edges, to get 2r>2 crossings** (extremal): If both layers delete the same r edges of the same cycle, the result is r disjoint cycles. If the deleted sets differ but have the same endpoint set, they must together form all of h, alternating, which forces the extreme value c_new = 2^{n-1}. With h_1 a translate of h_0 (swapping or fixing the two removed edges), the cyclic order of endpoints comes out wrong every time, so a single cycle never forms.
- **Splitting W = X+Y by naive alternating rounding of odd entries** (extremal): With ties among odd entries, the minimal m-subsets of X or Y need not be prefixes in W-order, and the bound can fall short by about half the tie-class size. I replaced this with the polymatroid-intersection argument.
- **Prism construction (zigzag over a single Hamiltonian cycle of Q_{n−1})** (extremal): Hamiltonian cycles of C_N × K_2 have only 2 or N rungs, so the new dimension gets only c ∈ {2, 2^{n-1}}.
- **Realize everything with $c_0=2m$ by gluing a Hamiltonian cycle $h$ in one half to its translate $h+t$ in the other half, splicing at m shared edges** (strategist): The resulting vectors are confined to the form $(2m, 2a-\sum 2e_{j_k})$, close to twice a single vector, so most targets are unreachable. For m≥2, splicing at shared edges whose relative orientation is the same (rather than crossed) produces several cycles, not one.
- **Two-edge crossed splicing to get $c_0=4$ in general** (strategist): It needs two realizations sharing two edges of prescribed dimensions with opposite relative orientation (C10-type condition). Translation freedom only guarantees one shared edge, so existence in general is unproved.
- **Look for further obstructions beyond admissibility using tight sets S (sum_S c_i = 2^{|S|}), which force h to be a lift of a Hamiltonian cycle of Q_S with each fibre traversed by a single Hamiltonian path** (construction): In the cases checked (n=5 with S carrying (4,2,2) and Q_2 fibres; n=6 with Q_3 fibres) the achievable T-counts match exactly the bounds already implied by admissibility, so no new obstruction appeared. Combined with the chain property of tight sets, this suggests tight sets give no extra constraint.
- **Find n=4 counterexamples to the recorded conditions** (construction): For n≤4 the recorded conditions coincide with admissibility, and all four admissible n=4 vectors are realized, so the first failures are at n=5.
- **Split $D$ so that both $X$ and $Y$ are non-decreasing in one fixed sorted order. Then only prefix conditions would need checking.** (construction): It can be infeasible. For $D=(2,3,3)$ the only valid splits are $(1,1,2)+(1,2,1)$, up to swapping $X$ and $Y$, and neither is monotone for both.
- **Alternate $\lceil\cdot\rceil/\lfloor\cdot\rfloor$ across all odd entries in one fixed sorted order and check only prefix sums of that order.** (construction): Inside a class of equal odd values, the sorted order of $X$ takes the floors first, so the prefixes of the fixed order are not the binding sets. This needs the concavity and parity estimate, and the class-start sign must be chosen from $\sigma_L$.
- **Assume the tight sets of $c$ form a chain (C-1adbba).** (construction): False for $c$ itself: two coordinates equal to 2 give disjoint tight singletons. The fix is to work with $d$ and $f=2^{|S|}-1$, where the tight sets are pairwise intersecting and closed under intersection and union. That is enough.
- **Lemma B for (4,14,6,4,4) via x=y=(8,4,2,2), j=0, β=1** (extremal): Every (8,4,2,2)-cycle alternates in d_0, so the gap of a d_0 edge and a d_1 edge is odd in both cycles. No twist is possible (proved). A different split, K=(8,2,2,4) with H1 and dims (2,3), works.
- **Lemma B with h_1 = vector-preserving automorphic image of H1 (translations by e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3; swap(1,2); swap(1,2)∘e_0) to get (10,8,6,4,4) or (10,6,6,6,4)** (extremal): Translation by e_3 and swap(1,2)∘e_0 fix H1. The others give cycles obtained from H1 by permuting common segments, all traversed forward, so every common edge has the same orientation (no twist).
- **Flip-gluing (h and flip(h)) starting from G or H1** (extremal): G (the RGC) and H1 are parity-rigid: each dimension's edges sit at positions of one parity, so they have no flippable squares at all.
- **Vertical flip on the reflection-glued (12,6,6,4|4) cycle to reach (10,6,6,6,4)** (extremal): All six d_0 edges common to both copies are traversed in opposite directions (gap even), so none is flippable.
- **Horizontal flip in the (12,4,8,4|4) cycle moving 2 from d_2 to d_0 to reach (14,...)** (extremal): All d_2 edges in both copies have b_0=0, so no {0,2}-square contains two d_2 edges.
- **Reflection gluing for (14,6,4,4,4), (10,8,6,4,4), (10,6,6,6,4)** (extremal): Reflection gluing needs w_α=w_γ and w_k≡0 mod 4 for the remaining k (w_k=2x_k with x_k even). Each of these vectors fails one of the two conditions.

## Orchestrator assessment

This was the second and final iteration. The question is open in general: we have a candidate answer, but sufficiency is unproved for $n\ge 6$.

**Necessary condition (P), reviewed and sound.** Every Hamiltonian cycle $h$ of $Q_n$ satisfies (P): $\sum_{i\in S}c_i(h)\ge 2^{|S|}$ for every nonempty $S$. Equivalently, the sorted entries satisfy $s_1+\dots+s_k\ge 2^k$ for every $k$. Four agents proved this independently, by projecting $h$ onto the coordinates in $S$ and counting runs (C-618835, C-41210c, C-7df5df, C-974f89, C-cfd4b4, C-97126c). Together with evenness and $\sum c_i=2^n$, (P) contains all the recorded conditions of Lemma 3.1 in arXiv:1009.3304: $|S|=1$ gives $c_i\ge 2$ and $|S|=n-1$ gives $\max c_i\le 2^{n-1}$.

**The recorded conditions are not sufficient for $n\ge5$, reviewed.**
- For $n=5$, the vectors $(2,2,2,10,16)$ and $(2,2,2,12,14)$ meet the recorded conditions but violate (P).
- For general $n\ge5$, a valid witness is $(2,\dots,2,\;2^{n-1}-6-2(n-5),\;2^{n-1})$ with $n-2$ entries equal to 2. More simply, no chromatic vector can have three entries equal to 2 when $n\ge3$.
- For $n\le4$, (P) adds nothing to the recorded conditions.

**Small cases.**
- $n\le4$: exhaustive enumeration (E-a0c349) shows the image is exactly the set of even vectors satisfying (P). Up to permutation: $\{(2,2)\}$, $\{(2,2,4)\}$, and $\{(2,2,4,8),(2,2,6,6),(2,4,4,6),(4,4,4,4)\}$. The cycle counts 1, 6 and 1344 match the known values, which is a good sanity check.
- $n=5$: CP-SAT (E-a0c349) found a Hamiltonian cycle for each of the 28 sorted (P)-vectors. The two excluded vectors are ruled out by the projection argument, not by the solver. The extremal agent also gave explicit constructions for the 9 vectors that gluing and lifting do not reach (C-a875ff). These have not been reviewed or machine-checked, but they agree with CP-SAT. So $\mathrm{Im}(5)=\mathcal P_5$ rests on the solver, with a hand construction as corroboration.

**Inductive tools.**
- Reviewed and sound: the lifting lemma (C-faefa5, $n\ge3$) and the halving lemma (C-15a42a, which replaces the recalled polymatroid splitting result).
- Stated as proved but not yet reviewed: the gluing lemma (C-f1bc65 / C-bc45df / C-ddc1e6) and the conditional theorem C-042591.
