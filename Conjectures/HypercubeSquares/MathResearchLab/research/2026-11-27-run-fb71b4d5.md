## Original conjecture

\begin{conjecture}[Inscribed squares in Hamiltonian cycles.] \label{c:squaresConjecture} Let $n \ge 2$ be an integer. No Hamiltonian cycle of $\hyp{n}$ is square free, or equivalently any Hamiltonian cycle has inscribed a square.
\end{conjecture}

**Normalized statement.** For every integer $n \ge 2$ and every Hamiltonian cycle $H$ of the hypercube $Q_n$, there exists a 4-cycle $v_0v_1v_2v_3$ of $Q_n$ such that $H$ contains both edges $v_0v_1$ and $v_2v_3$, i.e. two opposite edges of a 4-cycle of $Q_n$ (an inscribed square).

## Source

Feliú Sagols, Guillermo Morales-Luna, *Parity balance of the $i$-th dimension edges in Hamiltonian cycles of the hypercube* (2010), arXiv:1009.3304, doi:10.48550/arxiv.1009.3304

## Definitions

- **Hypercube $Q_n$**: The graph with vertex set $\{0,1\}^n$ in which two vertices are adjacent iff they differ in exactly one coordinate.
- **Inscribed (straight or twisted) square**: A 4-cycle $v_0v_1v_2v_3$ of $Q_n$ is inscribed in a Hamiltonian cycle $h=h_0\cdots h_{2^n-1}$ if there are indices $i,j$ with $h_ih_{i+1}h_jh_{j+1}=v_0v_1v_2v_3$ (straight) or $=v_0v_1v_3v_2$ (twisted); equivalently, both opposite edges $v_0v_1$ and $v_2v_3$ (the rims) lie on $h$. A Hamiltonian cycle with no inscribed square is square-free.

**Why the bound matters.** Fix a square-free Hamiltonian cycle $H$ of $Q_n$ and a dimension $d$. Let $S_d\subseteq V(Q_{n-1})$ be the set of points that $H$'s $d$-edges project to. Then:
- $S_d$ is independent. If two $d$-edges of $H$ project to adjacent points, they are the two rims of a 4-cycle.
- $S_d$ has as many even vertices as odd ones. $H$ restricted to one half $x_d=0$ splits into paths that cover a copy of $Q_{n-1}$, and $S_d$ is exactly the set of their endpoints. A path in a bipartite graph has (#even − #odd vertices) $=$ (#even − #odd endpoints)/2. Summing over the paths gives 0, because $Q_{n-1}$ is balanced.

**The case split.** A 4-cycle of $Q_{a+b}$ uses exactly two coordinate directions, each twice. Opposite edges of a 4-cycle are parallel, so a layer edge and a rung are never opposite. That leaves three cases:
- **Type A (both directions in $Q_a$):** the 4-cycle lies in one layer. The constructed cycle avoids these iff $P$ and $P'$ are each square-free as edge sets, meaning straight and twisted squares are both forbidden.
- **Mixed, layer-rim pair:** the rims are the same $Q_a$-edge $xx'$ in two layers $y\sim y'$. Adjacent layers always have opposite parity, so one carries $P$ and the other $P'$. These are avoided iff $E(P)\cap E(P')=\emptyset$.
- **Mixed, rung-rim pair:** this would need two rungs over the same $Q_b$-edge. That never happens, because each edge of $C$ carries exactly one rung.
- **Type B (both directions in $Q_b$):** the rims are rungs at the same point $x\in\{u,v\}$. The rungs at $u$ and at $v$ are the two alternating perfect matchings of $C$. A straight inscribed square of $C$ has both rims in the same matching, and a twisted one has them in different matchings. So these are avoided iff $C$ has no straight inscribed square.

**Answers to the specific questions.**
- Edge-disjointness of $P$ and $P'$ is enough. The stronger condition "no edge of $P$ opposite an edge of $P'$ in a $Q_a$-square" is not needed, because a $Q_a$-square lies inside a single layer.

**Additivity (C-569d84).** It holds via an explicit construction. Run the same layered pattern along a square-free $s$–$t$ path $R$ of $Q_b$ to get the first path. For the second path, use the other $Q_b$ path $R'$ and swap the roles of $P$ and $P'$. The two results are edge-disjoint, square-free, and both join $(u,s)$ to $(u,t)$, which are at distance $d(s,t)$ apart.

**Generalisation.** The rung points may vary from layer to layer. Square-freeness then reduces to three conditions:
- each layer path is square-free;
- the paths in $Q_b$-adjacent layers are edge-disjoint;
- for each point $x$, the set of $C$-edges carrying a rung at $x$ contains no two opposite edges of a $Q_b$-square.

## Failed approaches

- **Standard doubling: H in both layers of Q_{n+1}, delete a common edge ab and add rungs at a and b (and similar splicings with a common edge in adjacent layers).** (strategist): The rungs at the adjacent vertices a and b are parallel and adjacent, so they form an inscribed square. More generally the rung set must be independent (C9), and reusing an edge in adjacent layers is itself a square.
- **Pigeonhole via alpha_=(Q_{n-1}) for n=7 (as in Sagols–Morales-Luna) and for any n>=7.** (strategist): alpha_=(Q_6)>=20 by an explicit set, so 7·20>=128. alpha_= at least doubles per dimension, so the inequality n·alpha_=(Q_{n-1})<2^n fails for every n>=7.
- **Pure Cartesian product of square-free cycles (staircase 2-factor) as a Hamiltonian cycle.** (strategist): It always has sum gcd/2 >= 2 cycles (C11).
- **Merging cycles of a square-free 2-factor by 4-cycle switches.** (strategist): The added pair is a pair of opposite edges of the switched 4-cycle, which is an inscribed square.
- **Square-free perfect matching A of Q_4 plus a translate B=A+v to get a square-free 2-factor of Q_4.** (strategist): For the explicit matching tried, every admissible v gives adjacent projections (only two 8-cycles, with squares between A and B). In fact Q_4 has no square-free 2-factor at all, by C6.
- **Single-direction counting a_i ≤ α_=(Q_{n-1}), Σ a_i = 2^n, for n = 7** (extremal): α_=(Q_6) = 20 (proved here), and 7·20 = 140 ≥ 128 leaves slack 12; the paper's α_=(Q_6)=16 is wrong under the balanced-independent definition.
- **Same counting for any n ≥ 8** (extremal): α_=(Q_{n-1}) ≥ 2^{n-3} ≥ 2^n/n, and in fact α_=(Q_{n-1}) = 2^{n-2}(1−O(1/√n)), so the gap grows like a factor n/4. Counterexamples exist at n=8,9,10.
- **Plain independence bound a_i ≤ α(Q_{n-1}) = 2^{n-2}** (extremal): Settles only n ≤ 3, since n·2^{n-2} ≥ 2^n for n ≥ 4.
- **Inductive construction of square-free cycles by doubling (gluing Hamiltonian cycles of two copies of Q_n after deleting edges)** (extremal): By C7 the half-forests of a square-free cycle have pairwise non-adjacent endpoints, so they are never sub-forests of Hamiltonian cycles of Q_n; deleting an edge uv and crossing at u,v always creates the square u v v' u'.
- **Lift a square-free cycle $C$ of $Q_n$ to $Q_{n+1}$ by deleting an edge $uv$ in both layers and adding rungs at $u$ and $v$** (construction): The rungs at the adjacent vertices $u,v$ are rims of the square $u_0v_0v_1u_1$. Using the same path in both layers also makes every edge $xy$ form a square with its copy. The two-layer equivalence shows what is actually needed: two edge-disjoint square-free forests and an independent rung set.
- **Product lemma with inner dimension $a\le6$ (for example to get $Q_9..Q_{14}$ from $Q_8$)** (construction): Every layer needs a square-free Hamiltonian path of $Q_a$, and none exists for $2\le a\le6$. For $a=1$, adjacent layers would share the single edge.
- **Counting via $n\,\alpha_=(Q_{n-1})<2^n$ to prove the conjecture for $n=7$ or higher** (construction): $\alpha_=(Q_6)\ge20$ and $\alpha_=(Q_m)\ge20\cdot2^{m-6}$, so the inequality fails for every $n\ge7$.
- **Using reflected-Gray-code-like cycles as straight-free outer cycles** (construction): Any $i,j,i$ step pattern gives a straight square, and Gray codes are full of them.
- **Obtaining SF(11) from the two-path product lemma, either as $T(7)$ plus a straight-free cycle of $Q_4$ or by any other split** (extremal): $Q_4$ has no straight-free Hamiltonian cycle (proved here), and nor do $Q_2$ or $Q_3$. Assuming C-1c6bfc, $T(a)$ is false for $a\le6$, so no split $a+b=11$ works.
- **Merging cycles of a 2-factor by a 4-cycle switch to get a straight-free (rather than square-free) Hamiltonian cycle** (extremal): Removing rims $v_0v_1$ and $v_2v_3$ and adding $v_1v_2$ and $v_3v_0$ yields a cycle that traverses $v_1\to v_2$ and then $v_3\to v_0$. That is always a straight inscribed square, so switches cannot help even when twisted squares are allowed.
- **Using the translation $\tau_j$ (or any automorphism mapping a $j$-edge of $P$ onto a $j$-edge of $P$) to make $P'=\varphi(H-uv)$ edge-disjoint from $P$** (construction): $\tau_j$ fixes every direction-$j$ edge setwise, so $M_j(H)\setminus\{uv\}$ (nonempty, since $a_j\ge2$) is shared. For general automorphisms the expected overlap is about 64 edges, so this probably fails, but the cheap exhaustive check was requested.
- **Product lemma with a small outer cube ($b\le4$)** (construction): $Q_2$, $Q_3$ and $Q_4$ have no straight-free Hamiltonian cycle (C4). So outer factors need $b\ge5$.
- **Two-layer gluing ($b=1$) with adjacent endpoints, e.g. $P=H-uv$** (construction): With $u\sim v$ the two rungs are opposite sides of a 4-cycle. $T^*$ requires $d(u,v)\ge3$.
- **Forest-pair product with even $r$ (all direction counts $\equiv0\pmod4$)** (construction): The layered 2-factor splits into exactly $\gcd(r,2^{b-1})\ge2$ cycles, whatever $C$ is. Merging would need varying the forest pairs across layers, which is not attempted.
