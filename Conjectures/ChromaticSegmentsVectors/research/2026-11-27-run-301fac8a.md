## Original conjecture

{\em Characterize the chromatic-segments vectors of Hamiltonian cycles of $\hyp{n}$. } Or, in other words, {\em characterize, for each $i$, the image of the map $\delta_i:\{\mbox{Hamiltonian cycles}\}\to\N^n$.}

**Normalized statement.** For each $n\ge2$ and $i\in\{0,\dots,n-1\}$, determine exactly which finite lists of positive integers arise as $\delta_i(h)=[j_1-j_0,j_2-j_1,\dots,2^n-j_{c_i-1}]$ for a Hamiltonian cycle $h$ of $Q_n$. Here $[j_0=0,\dots,j_{c_i-1}]$ are the positions at which $i$-th dimension edges start in $h$, normalized so that $h_0h_1$ is an $i$-th dimension edge with $(h_0)_i=0$.

## Source

Feliú Sagols, Guillermo Morales-Luna, *Parity balance of the $i$-th dimension edges in Hamiltonian cycles of the hypercube* (2010), arXiv:1009.3304, doi:10.48550/arxiv.1009.3304

## Definitions

- **chromatic-segments list $\delta_i(h)$**: $[j_1-j_0,\dots,2^n-j_{c_i-1}]$, where $\lambda_i(h)=[j_0,\dots,j_{c_i-1}]$ lists the indices of $h$ at which $i$-th dimension edges start. Its entries are the gaps between consecutive $i$-th dimension edges.

## Established facts

*Known results recorded with citations:*

- The entries of $\delta_i(h)$ at even positions sum to $2^{n-1}$, and so do the entries at odd positions. (Feliú Sagols, Guillermo Morales-Luna, Parity balance of the $i$-th dimension edges in Hamiltonian cycles of the hypercube, arXiv:1009.3304 (Corollary 4.2, p. 5); computational: False; reproduced: None)

## Approaches attempted

- **construction** (iteration 1, main): I worked with the reformulation C4: a list is realizable iff there are distinct vertices $x_0,\dots,x_{c-1}$ of $Q_{n-1}$ and paths $P_k$ from $x_k$ to $x_{k+1}$ with $g_k$ vertices, such that the even-indexed paths partition $V(Q_{n-1})$ and so do the odd-indexed ones. - **[C-b8f052]** (lemma; by construction; author's support: proved_here; level: written proof attempt)
  (Parity balance) With $j_k=g_0+\dots+g_{k-1}$, every realizable $\delta_i(h)$ satisfies $\sum_{k=0}^{c-1}(-1)^{k+j_k}=0$. A path with both endpoints of colour $\alpha$ has one extra vertex of colour $\alpha$, and a path with mixed endpoints is balanced. Balance of $H_1$ therefore means equally many endpoints of each colour. The $H_1$-endpoint of the $k$-th $i$-edge is $h_{j_k+1}$ for $k$ even and $h_{j_k}$ for $k$ odd, with colour $\equiv j_k+[k \text{ even}]\equiv j_k+k+1$. Hence $\sum_k(-1)^{j_k+k}=0$.

### [P-f04086] Projection to $Q_{n-1}$ and bipartite counting

By agent:strategist. Status **awaiting_review**, level written proof attempt.

**Target:** Necessity of (A)–(D) for all $n\ge2$

Use the C1 formulation. (A),(B),(C) are immediate. For (D), colour vertices by position parity and count even positions in half 1. Each odd-length segment contributes an imbalance $\pm1$ with sign $-(-1)^{j_k}$, and the half (a copy of $Q_{n-1}$) is colour-balanced, so the signed sum vanishes. The half-0 condition is equivalent because the whole cycle alternates colours.

## Failed approaches

- **Extending the $n=4$ obstruction (forced vertex degree exceeding $n-1$, or forced triangles) to an infinite family for all $n$** (construction): In the projected model each vertex of $Q_{n-1}$ needs at most 4 forced distinct neighbours, and $Q_{n-1}$ has degree $\ge4$ for $n\ge5$. Triangle obstructions only change which interior vertex goes where; they don't rule a list out. Two vertices can be forced to share at most 2 neighbours, so no $K_{2,3}$ can be forced either.
- **Looking for an $n=5$ analogue of C5: $c=14$, one 4-gap in half 1 and two 3-gaps in half 0 not adjacent to it** (construction): The colour bookkeeping forces a consistent assignment: the 3-gap closer after the 4-gap takes $w$ and the other takes $u$. Degree 4 in $Q_4$ then exactly accommodates the forced neighbours, so no contradiction appears. The case still needs a computer check (experiment 2).
- **Conjecture that (A)–(D) (or just the alternating-sum condition plus parity) characterises $\mathrm{Im}_n$ for all $n$** (strategist): $[3,2,3,4,2,2]$ satisfies (A)–(D) but is not in $\mathrm{Im}_4$, because of a degree-3 obstruction in $Q_3$ (C6).
- **Generate $\mathrm{Im}_n$ from $\mathrm{Im}_{n-1}$ using only single gluings along one other dimension (G1/G2)** (strategist): The result depends on choosing the edge class. $[3,2,3,3,2,3]\in\mathrm{Im}_4$, but every single gluing of $\mathrm{Im}_3$ lists leaves an entry 4 untouched. For $c=4$, the class $R_n$ ($a=N/2\pm1$, $b\ne N/2$ even) is never produced.
- **Realise $\delta$ inside the prism subgraph $C_{2^{n-1}}\times K_2\subseteq Q_n$ ($K_2$ in dimension $i$)** (strategist): As I recall, the prism's Hamiltonian cycles use either two adjacent rungs or every rung (zigzag), giving only $[N,N]$ and $[2,\dots,2]$; I did not prove this. Richer subgraphs, such as tori $C_M\times C_4$, have not been analysed.
- **Build both path covers $P_0,P_1$ as arcs of a single Hamiltonian cycle $C$ of $Q_{n-1}$** (extremal): Arcs of at least 2 vertices mean the deleted edge sets $F_0,F_1$ are matchings of $C$ with $V(F_0)=V(F_1)$. This forces $F_0=F_1$ unless $V(F_0)=V(C)$. If $F_0=F_1$, each path pairs with its own copy, giving $c/2$ separate cycles. So only $c=2$ and $c=2^{n-1}$ arise this way.
- **Conjecture that (C1)–(C4) characterize $D_n$** (extremal): Fails at $n=4$: $[3,2,3,4,2,2]$.
- **Generate $D_n$ from $D_{n-1}$ by the doubling lemma alone** (extremal): Doubling creates no new odd entries; e.g. $[3,2,5,6]\in D_4$ is not obtained from $D_3$.
