## Failed approaches

- **Use a finite collection of square-residue tests to isolate $n=4$.** (extremal): For any nonempty finite collection of moduli $m_i\ge2$, put $M=\operatorname{lcm}(2,m_1,\ldots,m_s)$. Every $n\ge4$ with $n\equiv4\pmod M$ has $D_n\equiv D_4=9\pmod{m_i}$ for every $i$, by periodicity of $F_n$ and equality of parity. Thus a full progression of density $1/M$ passes every one of these tests.
- **Rule out perfect powers with congruence or power-residue sieving modulo a fixed modulus (e.g. squares mod 8, or $k$-th power residues mod primes $p\equiv1 \pmod k$).** (construction): For even $n\equiv0\pmod{2M}$, $D(n)\equiv1\pmod{2M}$ (C6). So every modulus leaves an infinite progression open. Also, $D(n)\equiv1\pmod8$ for all even $n$, so squares are not excluded mod 8.
- **2-adic obstruction for $n\equiv5\pmod8$, where only $k=2$ is possible: require $D(n)/4\equiv1\pmod8$.** (construction): It works for $n=5$ ($D(5)/4=11\equiv3 \pmod 8$), but $n=13$ gives $D(13)/4=572698233\equiv1\pmod8$, so this class is not uniformly excluded.
- **An abc-conjecture argument, as for Brocard's problem: write $a^k=nD(n-1)\pm1$.** (construction): $D(n-1)$ is not smooth, so the radical of $nD(n-1)$ can be as large as $D(n)$ itself. abc then gives no useful bound, unlike the case of $n!$.
- **Covering congruences / local obstruction modulo a fixed $M$ (as in the proof that $1!+\dots+n!$ is a power only for $n=1,3$)** (strategist): $D(n)\bmod M$ is purely periodic (period dividing $2M$) and attains $0$, $1$ and $9$ on the classes $n\equiv1,2,4\pmod{2M}$ (C2). Unlike sums of factorials, it never stabilizes.
- **Erdős–Selfridge-type arguments (products of consecutive integers are never powers)** (strategist): $D(n)$ has no product structure beyond the factor $n-1$; its cofactor $E(n)$ is not a product of consecutive integers.
- **abc conjecture (as in Overholt's conditional finiteness for Brocard)** (strategist): Brocard benefits from $\mathrm{rad}(n!)\approx e^n\ll n!$. In $D(n)=nD(n-1)\pm1$ there is no reason for $\mathrm{rad}(nD(n-1))$ to be small, so abc gives a trivial bound.
- **Size/Diophantine approximation: $|D(n)-n!/e|<1/(n+1)$** (strategist): This would require approximation results for $e\cdot y^k$ near factorials, far beyond current transcendence methods.
- **Treating $F$ as a convergent power series around $x=1$ and reading off Taylor coefficients (the framing of C-55c254)** (extremal): Convergence of the rearranged Stirling-number series in $\mathbb Z_3$ is not obvious, because $|a_j|\approx3^{-j/2}$ sits at the boundary of Amice's analyticity criterion. I replaced it with the Lipschitz/isometry argument, which avoids analyticity entirely.
- **Hoping for a single value of $v_3(D(n))$ when $v_3(n-1)=2$** (extremal): At first order, $G(t)\approx G(0)+t$, and both terms have valuation 2, so they can cancel. They do cancel exactly on the class $n\equiv19\pmod{27}$, where $v_3$ is unbounded because of the second 3-adic zero of $F$.
